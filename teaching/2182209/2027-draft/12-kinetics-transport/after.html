Two more worked examples

1. Butler-Volmer at a larger push. With f = F/RT = 38.92 V−1, α = 0.5 and η = −0.150 V, the exponent is αfη = 0.5 × 38.92 × (−0.150) = −2.92:

j = j0[e−2.92 − e+2.92] = 1.00 × (0.054 − 18.52) = −18.5 mA/cm²

At this η the oxidation term (0.054) is negligible. That is the Tafel region, where each tenfold rise in current costs about 118 mV.

2. Kinetics and transport together. If transport limits the same electrode to jlim = 6 mA/cm², the slower step dominates:

1/|j| = 1/18.5 + 1/6 = 0.054 + 0.167 = 0.221, so |j| = 4.5 mA/cm²

The kinetics could give 18.5, but the ions arrive only fast enough for 4.5. Pushing η further would gain little; stirring would help.

Three mistakes to avoid

  1. "At η = 0 nothing happens." Both directions run at j0; only the net current is zero.
  2. "Double η, double the current." The current grows exponentially: doubling η from −0.05 to −0.10 V triples it here.
  3. "More voltage always plates faster." Near jlim the extra voltage goes mostly into side reactions such as H2, and the deposit grows rough.

Practice

Work on paper. The first four repeat the class; the rest go one step further. j = j0[eαfη − e−αfη], f = 38.92 V−1, α = 0.5; 1/|j| = 1/|jk| + 1/jlim; jlim = nFDc/δ; F = 96 485 C/mol.

1. Find j at η = −0.050 V with j0 = 1.00 mA/cm².

Answer1.00 × (e−0.973 − e0.973) = −2.268 mA/cm².

2. η doubles to −0.100 V. Does j double?

AnswerNo: −6.858 mA/cm², 3.0 times more.

3. With jlim = 2 mA/cm², η goes from −0.20 to −0.25 V. By how much does |j| rise?

AnswerFrom 1.922 to 1.970 mA/cm²: about 2.5%.

4. Name two ways to raise jlim in a plating bath.

AnswerStir or spin (thinner δ); raise the concentration; warm the bath (larger D).

5. Find j at η = −0.150 V and at −0.200 V (j0 = 1.00).

Answer−18.5 and −49.0 mA/cm².

6. From your answers to 5, what is the ratio for 50 mV more? Compare with the Tafel slope of 118 mV per decade.

Answer49.0 / 18.5 = 2.65. A factor of 10 would need 118 mV; 50 mV gives 1050/118 = 2.65. They agree.

7. Faster spinning thins the diffusion layer from 50 µm to 10 µm. Estimate jlim for 0.6 M Cu2+, D = 7 × 10−6 cm²/s.

Answer2 × 96 485 × 7 × 10−6 × 0.6 × 10−3 / 10 × 10−4 = 0.81 A/cm² = 810 mA/cm², five times the 50 µm value.

8. Kinetics alone would give 18.5 mA/cm², and jlim = 6 mA/cm². Find |j|. Which step limits?

Answer1/|j| = 1/18.5 + 1/6, so |j| = 4.5 mA/cm². Transport limits.
