The question for Class 12 3 min

Class 9 told us whether a reaction can go. A plating engineer also needs to know how fast it goes, and what it costs to make it go faster. Class 12 has two answers: the reaction at the surface needs an extra push in voltage, and the ions must reach the surface in time.

Current density is a rate 5 min

Every Cu atom plated takes 2 electrons, so the current tells you how fast Cu is plated. Per unit area, the current density j (in mA/cm²) is a reaction rate:

rate in mol per cm² per s = j / (nF)

In this course, cathodic currents (reduction, as in plating) are written negative.

Overpotential: the extra push 7 min

At the Nernst potential Eeq (Class 9) there is no net current. Recall Class 5: at equilibrium the reaction does not stop; it runs both ways at equal rates. At an electrode, Cu2+ + 2e− ⇌ Cu still runs both ways. The current that flows each way at equilibrium is the exchange current density j0.

To get a net current you must move the potential away from Eeq. The difference is the overpotential:

η = E − Eeq. Make η negative and reduction wins.

Overpotential is wasted voltage: in a battery it lowers the voltage you get; in plating it raises the voltage you pay. An electrode with a large j0 (a "fast" electrode) needs little η.

The ions have to get there 3 min

Plating uses up Cu2+ at the surface, so more must diffuse in from the bulk solution through a thin layer near the electrode. If the reaction is faster than the supply, the surface runs out of Cu2+, and the current cannot rise any further: the limiting current. Stirring, a more concentrated bath and a warmer bath all raise it.

Check yourself 5 min

Write your answers first, then open each one.

1. At equilibrium, has the reaction at the electrode stopped? (Class 5)

AnswerNo. Both directions run at equal rates; the current each way is j0.

2. Eeq = 0.2808 V and the electrode is held at 0.2308 V. What is η?

Answerη = 0.2308 − 0.2808 = −0.050 V: reduction is driven.

3. e2 ≈ 7.4. Roughly what is e4?

AnswerAbout 55.

4. Which needs more overpotential for the same current, a large or a small j0?

AnswerA small j0: the slow electrode.

5. What sets the thickness of plated Cu: current, or current density and time? (Class 10)

AnswerCurrent density and time.

6. Name one way to raise the limiting current.

AnswerStir or spin; raise the concentration; warm the bath.
