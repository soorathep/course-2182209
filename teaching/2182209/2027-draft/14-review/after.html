Mixed practice, in exam style

Each question uses more than one class. Work on paper, with units. Constants: NA = 6.022 × 1023 mol−1, F = 96 485 C/mol, e = 1.602 × 10−19 C; M: Cu 63.55, Si 28.09, O 16.00 g/mol; Cu density 8.96 g/cm³.

1. (Classes 1, 3, 7) How many Cu2+ ions are in 25.0 mL of 0.200 M CuSO4, and how many electrons are needed to plate them all?

Answern = 5.00 × 10−3 mol = 3.01 × 1021 ions; electrons 2 × that = 6.02 × 1021 (0.0100 mol).

2. (Classes 4, 10) That solution is plated out completely at 0.500 A and 100% efficiency. How long does it take?

AnswerQ = 0.0100 × 96 485 = 965 C; t = 965 / 0.500 = 1930 s, about 32 min.

3. (Classes 9, 10) As the bath in question 2 is used up, [Cu2+] falls from 0.200 M to 0.0020 M. By how much does E(Cu2+/Cu) change?

AnswerTwo decades: 2 × 0.0296 = 0.059 V lower.

4. (Classes 2, 6) A Si layer doped with 1016 cm−3 of P has ρ = 0.46 Ω·cm. Find R for a strip 100 µm long, 10 µm wide and 1 µm thick.

AnswerR = ρL/A = 0.46 × 0.0100 cm / (1.0 × 10−3 × 1.0 × 10−4 cm²) = 4.6 × 104 Ω.

5. (Classes 3, 7) Thermal oxidation turns 44 nm of Si into 100 nm of SiO2. Which element is oxidized, and how many electrons does each Si atom lose?

AnswerSi, from 0 to +4: 4 electrons per Si. O goes from 0 to −2 (reduced).

6. (Classes 8, 10, 11) A sensor needs 0.50 W for 30 s, once a minute, for years. Compare a 10 F, 2.7 V supercapacitor and a 50 mAh, 3.7 V cell limited to 1C.

AnswerLoad: 15 J per pulse. Supercap: 36 J and plenty of power, and it survives hundreds of thousands of cycles: fits. Battery: 666 J, but 1C = 0.050 A × 3.7 V = 0.19 W, below 0.50 W: does not fit.

7. (Classes 12, 13) A Cu plating bath at pH 1 is run at 90% of its limiting current. Explain what the Pourbaix map says about Cu2+ in the bath, and why running so close to jlim is a bad idea.

AnswerAt pH 1 and potentials above 0.16 V Cu2+ stays dissolved, so the bath is stable. Near jlim, Cu2+ runs short at the surface; extra voltage goes to H2, the efficiency falls and the deposit grows rough.

8. (Classes 5, 9) Why does the Nernst equation use log10, like pH? What does one decade of concentration change in each?

AnswerBoth measure concentrations that span many powers of ten. One decade changes pH by 1 unit, and E by 0.0592/n V at 25 °C.
