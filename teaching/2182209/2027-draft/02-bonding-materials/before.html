The question for Class 2 3 min

In Class 1 you counted electrons. Class 2 asks what those electrons do in a solid. A chip needs three kinds of material: Cu, a wire that carries current easily; SiO2, an insulator that stops it; and Si, a semiconductor in between. Cu and SiO2 differ in how well they conduct by a factor of about 1024. The reason is where the outer electrons sit: free to move through the whole solid, or held in place.

Outer electrons do the bonding 4 min

Only the outer electrons, called valence electrons, take part in bonding. For the main-group elements you can read their number from the group in the periodic table:

ElementGroupValence electrons
Na11
Al, B133
C, Si144
N, P155
O166
Cl, F177

Si has 4 valence electrons, so each Si atom can form 4 bonds. You will use this again in Class 6, when B (3) and P (5) replace Si.

Three ways atoms hold together 7 min

  • Metallic (Cu, Al, W). Each atom gives some outer electrons to the whole solid. These electrons are not tied to any one atom, so they move when a voltage is applied. Metals conduct.
  • Covalent (Si, SiO2, diamond). Neighboring atoms share pairs of electrons in bonds. The electrons stay in their bonds, so few or none can move. In a network solid such as SiO2, the bonds run through the whole crystal; there are no separate molecules.
  • Ionic (NaCl). One atom takes electrons from another, forming ions that attract each other. The ions are held in place in the crystal, so solid NaCl does not conduct. Melt it or dissolve it, and the ions can move.
What decides conduction is whether charge carriers are free to move, not whether the solid contains charged particles.

Electronegativity sorts the bonds 6 min

Electronegativity, written χ (chi), measures how strongly an atom pulls shared electrons toward itself. Some values: Na 0.9, Al 1.5, Si 1.8, Cu 1.9, H 2.1, C 2.5, Cl 3.0, O 3.5, F 4.0.

The difference Δχ between two bonded atoms gives a rough guide:

ΔχBondExample
below 0.4nonpolar covalent: shared equallySi–Si, 0
0.4 to 1.8polar covalent: shared unequallySi–O, 1.7
above 1.8ionic: one atom takes the electronsNa–Cl, 2.1

Two metal atoms bond metallically, whatever their Δχ. The cut-offs are a guide with exceptions, not a law.

Resistivity and resistance 5 min

Resistivity ρ (rho), in Ω·m, is a property of the material. Resistance R, in Ω, depends on the material and on the shape of the piece:

R = ρ L / A, where L is the length and A the cross-sectional area

A longer wire resists more; a thicker wire resists less. Typical values: Cu 1.68 × 10−8 Ω·m, Si about 2 × 103 Ω·m, SiO2 about 1016 Ω·m.

Powers of ten. To compare two numbers this far apart, divide them and take log10: 1016 / (1.68 × 10−8) = 6 × 1023, or about 24 orders of magnitude.

Check yourself 5 min

Write your answers first, then open each one.

1. How many valence electrons do Si and O have?

AnswerSi 4 (group 14), O 6 (group 16).

2. Δχ for Na–Cl is 3.0 − 0.9. What kind of bond is it?

Answer2.1, above 1.8: ionic.

3. Solid NaCl contains ions. Does it conduct?

AnswerNo. The ions are held in place. It conducts only when molten or dissolved.

4. Which electrons carry the current in a Cu wire?

AnswerThe outer electrons that belong to the whole solid, not to any one atom.

5. A wire is made twice as long. What happens to R?

AnswerIt doubles: R is proportional to L.

6. Which is the material property: R or ρ?

Answerρ. R also depends on the length and area of the piece.
