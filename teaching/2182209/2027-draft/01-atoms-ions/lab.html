Counting particles in chip and battery materials

About 30 minutes. Works in class or on your own, on a laptop or a phone. Nothing to install; a calculator helps.

How this lab works

A chip uses Si, SiO2 and Cu; a Li-ion battery uses Li+ and Cu foil; a plating bath holds Cu2+. You count the particles in these species, calculate the atomic mass of Cu from its isotopes, find the charge of Cu in its two oxides, and place the three chip materials. Write each prediction before you look; the next step stays locked until you do. Numbers are checked against the expected value; after two tries the expected value is shown.

What runs where. The tables on this page come from atoms_lab.py, computed in advance. The slider in Task 3 does one small calculation in JavaScript, the same weighted average as the Python. No Python is installed or run on your device. To run the Python yourself, open the slides and press D on a slide marked “D · Python” (it runs in your browser), or run atoms_lab.py in desktop Python.
