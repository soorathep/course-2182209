Why a chip course starts with atoms 3 min

A computer chip is built from a few materials, each with one job. The silicon (Si) wafer holds the transistors. Silicon dioxide (SiO2) insulates one part from another. Copper (Cu) wires carry current between the transistors. In the plating bath that makes those wires, copper is not metal but the ion Cu2+.

Why Cu conducts, why SiO2 does not, and how Cu2+ turns into Cu metal all come down to one question: how many electrons each atom or ion has. Class 1 teaches you to count them.

The three particles in an atom 5 min

e is the elementary charge, 1.602 × 10−19 C. u is the atomic mass unit, 1.6605 × 10−27 kg. Protons and neutrons are about 1840 times heavier than an electron, so almost all the mass sits in the tiny nucleus.

In chemical reactions, in plating and in batteries, only electrons are gained or lost. The nucleus does not change.

Atomic number, mass number and a symbol 5 min

  • Atomic number Z = number of protons. Z identifies the element: every copper atom has Z = 29, every silicon atom Z = 14.
  • Mass number A = protons + neutrons. So neutrons = A − Z.

Up to four numbers can sit around a symbol. In 6529Cu2+:

PositionMeaningHere
Top leftMass number A65
Bottom leftAtomic number Z (often left out)29
Top rightNet charge2+
Bottom rightNumber of atoms in a formula, as in SiO2none

So 65Cu2+ has 29 protons, 65 − 29 = 36 neutrons, and a charge of +2.

Ions: atoms with electrons added or removed 7 min

One rule gives every count:

net charge = protons − electrons

Worked example. How many electrons does Cu2+ have? Cu has Z = 29, so 29 protons. The charge is +2, so 29 − electrons = +2, and electrons = 27. The ion has lost two electrons and is still copper, because the 29 protons are unchanged.

Worked example. How many electrons does O2− have? O has Z = 8. 8 − electrons = −2, so electrons = 10. A negative ion has more electrons than its atom.

A positive ion is a cation (Li+, Na+, Cu2+). A negative ion is an anion (O2−, F−, Cl−). The most common error in this class is to give Cu2+ 31 electrons. Check it with the rule: 29 − 31 = −2, the wrong sign.

Formulas: subscripts count atoms 5 min

An element has one kind of atom: Cu, Si, and also O2, a molecule of two O atoms. A compound has two or more elements in a fixed ratio: SiO2 (Si : O = 1 : 2) and CuSO4 (Cu : S : O = 1 : 1 : 4).

The 2 in O2 is a subscript: it counts atoms. The 2+ in Cu2+ is a charge: it counts missing electrons. They mean different things.

Counting electrons in a formula unit. Add the electrons of each atom. One neutral SiO2 unit has 14 + 2 × 8 = 30 electrons.

Check yourself 5 min

Write your answers first, then open each one.

1. How many protons and electrons does Na+ have? Z(Na) = 11.

Answer11 protons and 10 electrons: 11 − 10 = +1.

2. An ion has 8 protons and 10 electrons. Which ion is it?

AnswerZ = 8 is oxygen, and 8 − 10 = −2: O2−.

3. How many neutrons are in 63Cu?

Answer63 − 29 = 34.

4. Element or compound: Cu, O2, SiO2?

AnswerCu and O2 are elements; SiO2 is a compound.

5. Cu loses two electrons. Is it still copper?

AnswerYes. The element is set by its 29 protons, and losing electrons does not change the nucleus. It is now Cu2+.

6. What is the difference between the 2 in O2 and the 2 in Cu2+?

AnswerIn O2 it counts atoms in the molecule. In Cu2+ it is the charge: two electrons fewer than protons.

If you got 5 or 6 right, you are ready. If not, reread the section on ions; Class 1 starts from it.
