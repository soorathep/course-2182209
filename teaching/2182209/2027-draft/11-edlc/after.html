Two more worked examples

1. A coin-size supercapacitor. A 10 F cell is charged at 0.50 A. The voltage rises at 0.050 V/s, which confirms C = I / (ΔV/Δt) = 0.50 / 0.050 = 10 F. At its rated 2.7 V it holds Q = 10 × 2.7 = 27 C and E = ½ × 10 × 2.7² = 36 J. In water it could run only to about 1.0 V, storing 5 J: 2.7² = 7.3 times less.

2. A transistor gate as a capacitor. A gate of 1 µm × 1 µm sits on 2.0 nm of SiO2 (ε = 3.9 × 8.854 × 10−12 F/m):

C = εA/d = 3.9 × 8.854 × 10−12 × 1.0 × 10−12 / 2.0 × 10−9 = 1.7 × 10−14 F = 17 fF

Chip makers replaced SiO2 with HfO2 (Class 1 practice), whose ε is about 6 times larger, to keep C high without making the oxide so thin that electrons leak through.

Three mistakes to avoid

  1. "A supercapacitor is a battery that charges fast." An EDLC stores charge by ions at a surface; nothing reacts, so Faraday's law does not give its capacity.
  2. Using V/t at one point. The step at t = 0 comes from internal resistance. Use the slope of the straight part.
  3. Forgetting the square. E = ½CV²: doubling V gives four times the energy.

Practice

Work on paper. The first four repeat the class; the rest go one step further. e = 1.602 × 10−19 C.

1. At 0.010 A, V rises at 0.050 V/s. Find C.

Answer0.010 / 0.050 = 0.200 F.

2. Capacitor A (0.200 F) is charged to 1.0 V. Find Q and E.

Answer0.200 C; 0.100 J.

3. The same charge sits on 0.400 F. Find V and E.

Answer0.500 V; 0.050 J.

4. During discharge, which keeps a nearly constant voltage: a battery or an EDLC?

AnswerA battery. An EDLC's V falls in a straight line, V = Q/C.

5. A 10 F cell charges at 0.50 A. How long to reach 2.7 V from 0? (Ignore the resistance step.)

AnswerQ = 27 C; t = Q / I = 54 s.

6. What voltage gives capacitor B (0.100 F) the same energy as A at 1.0 V?

Answer½ × 0.100 × V² = 0.100 J, so V = 1.414 V.

7. A DRAM cell is 25 fF at 1.1 V. How many electrons does it hold?

AnswerQ = 2.75 × 10−14 C; ÷ e = 1.7 × 105 electrons.

8. Find C for a 1 µm × 1 µm gate on 2.0 nm SiO2. What happens to C if the oxide is made 1.0 nm?

Answer17 fF; halving d doubles C, to 35 fF.
