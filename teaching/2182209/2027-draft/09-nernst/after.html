Two more worked examples

1. A Cu–Ag cell. Cu | Cu2+ || Ag+ | Ag. Ag+ has the higher E°, so it is reduced at the cathode.

E°cell = 0.80 − 0.34 = 0.46 V. The reaction Cu + 2 Ag+ → Cu2+ + 2 Ag moves n = 2 electrons, so ΔG° = −2 × 96 485 × 0.46 = −88.8 kJ/mol. Note that E°(Ag+/Ag) is not doubled although the equation has 2 Ag+.

2. A one-electron couple. For Ag+ + e− → Ag, n = 1, so E shifts 0.0592 V per tenfold change. At [Ag+] = 0.010 M:

E = 0.80 + 0.0592 × log10(0.010) = 0.80 − 0.118 = 0.682 V

The shift is twice that of Cu2+/Cu for the same dilution, because n is half as large.

Three mistakes to avoid

  1. "Multiply the equation, multiply E°." E° is energy per charge and does not scale; ΔG° does.
  2. Subtracting in the wrong order. E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode, with both written as reductions.
  3. Getting the sign of the shift wrong. Less Cu2+ means less drive to take electrons: E(Cu2+/Cu) falls on dilution.

Practice

Work on paper. The first four repeat the class; the rest go one step further. Use the E° table above; F = 96 485 C/mol; 0.0592/n V per decade at 25 °C.

1. Find E°cell for Fe | Fe2+ || Cu2+ | Cu.

Answer0.34 − (−0.44) = 0.78 V.

2. A cell has E°cell = −0.40 V as written. Is ΔG° positive or negative? Does it go?

AnswerΔG° > 0: it does not go as written; the reverse does.

3. Find E(Cu2+/Cu) at 0.010 M.

Answer0.34 + 0.0296 × (−2) = 0.2808 V.

4. What is E°cell for 2 Al + 3 Cu2+ → 2 Al3+ + 3 Cu?

Answer0.34 − (−1.66) = 2.00 V. E° is not multiplied.

5. Find E°cell and ΔG° for Cu | Cu2+ || Ag+ | Ag.

Answer0.46 V; ΔG° = −2 × 96 485 × 0.46 = −88.8 kJ/mol.

6. Find E°cell and ΔG° for Zn | Zn2+ || Ag+ | Ag.

Answer0.80 − (−0.76) = 1.56 V; Zn + 2 Ag+, n = 2; ΔG° = −301 kJ/mol.

7. A plating bath drops to [Cu2+] = 1.0 × 10−4 M near the wafer. Find E(Cu2+/Cu) there.

Answer0.34 + 0.0296 × (−4) = 0.222 V.

8. A Daniell cell has [Zn2+] = 0.10 M and [Cu2+] = 1.0 M. Find Ecell. Is it above or below 1.10 V, and why?

AnswerE = 1.10 − 0.0296 log10(0.10/1.0) = 1.10 + 0.0296 = 1.13 V. Above, because there is more Cu2+ to reduce and less Zn2+ product.
