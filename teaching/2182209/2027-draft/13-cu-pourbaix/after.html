Two more worked examples

1. Boundaries at pH 11. Put pH = 11 into the two sloped lines:

Cu/Cu2O: E = 0.472 − 0.0592 × 11 = −0.179 V
Cu2O/CuO: E = 0.641 − 0.0592 × 11 = −0.010 V

So at pH 11, Cu metal is stable only below −0.18 V; CuO is stable above about 0 V. In alkaline water, Cu oxidizes more easily than in neutral water.

2. Moving the vertical line. CuO + 2H+ → Cu2+ + H2O. Its equilibrium gives log10[Cu2+] + 2 pH = constant. At 10−6 M the line is at pH 6.67, so the constant is −6 + 13.34 = 7.34. At 10−4 M:

pH = (7.34 + 4) / 2 = 5.67

A 100 times higher dissolved Cu moves the line 1 pH unit to the left. The map depends on the concentration chosen.

Three mistakes to avoid

  1. "The diagram shows CuO, so a protective film forms quickly." It shows stability only: not rate (Class 12) and not whether the film protects.
  2. Reading the axes the wrong way. Up is more oxidizing; right is more alkaline.
  3. Trusting it outside its model. This diagram leaves out Cu(I) ions, cuprate ions above about pH 12, and additives such as NH3 or BTA.

Practice

Work on paper. The first four repeat the class; the rest go one step further. Lines: Cu2+/Cu at 0.162 V; Cu/Cu2O E = 0.472 − 0.0592 pH; Cu2O/CuO E = 0.641 − 0.0592 pH; CuO/Cu2+ at pH 6.67. Water: H2 line E = −0.0592 pH; O2 line E = 1.23 − 0.0592 pH.

1. Which form is stable at pH 3, +0.50 V? At pH 9, +0.40 V?

AnswerCu2+; CuO.

2. At pH 9, as E rises from −0.30 V, in what order do the forms appear?

AnswerCu, then Cu2O above −0.060 V, then CuO above +0.109 V.

3. Why does Cu not dissolve in an acid with no oxidizer?

AnswerPart of the Cu region lies above the H+/H2 line, so H+ cannot oxidize Cu.

4. Name the regions of immunity, corrosion and passivation for Cu.

AnswerCu: immunity; Cu2+: corrosion; Cu2O and CuO: passivation.

5. Find both sloped boundaries at pH 11.

AnswerCu/Cu2O: −0.179 V; Cu2O/CuO: −0.010 V.

6. Find the H2 and O2 water lines at pH 7. Is CuO at pH 7, +0.80 V inside the region where water is stable?

AnswerH2: −0.414 V; O2: 0.816 V. Yes, +0.80 V lies just below the O2 line.

7. At what pH does CuO start to dissolve if the dissolved Cu is 10−4 M instead of 10−6 M?

AnswerpH = (7.34 + 4) / 2 = 5.67.

8. A slurry engineer wants a soft oxide at pH 9 that the pad can remove. What potential range should the slurry's oxidizer give, by the diagram? What else must be checked?

AnswerAbove +0.109 V for CuO (or between −0.060 and +0.109 V for Cu2O), and below the O2 line (0.70 V at pH 9). The diagram cannot say how fast the oxide grows, whether it is soft enough to polish, or what complexing agents in the slurry do.
