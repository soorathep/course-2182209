The question for Class 13 3 min

A Cu wire meets many liquids during chip making: an acid plating bath, rinse water with air in it, an alkaline polishing slurry. In each one, Cu might stay as metal, dissolve as Cu2+, or grow an oxide. Class 13 puts all of these cases on one map, drawn from the Nernst equation of Class 9 with pH from Class 5.

The four forms of Cu 5 min

Going from Cu to Cu2O or CuO is an oxidation, so it happens at higher potentials. Going from CuO to Cu2+ keeps the oxidation number at +2; it is dissolution by acid, which depends on pH.

Two axes: potential and pH 7 min

A Pourbaix diagram plots potential E (vertical, against the SHE) against pH (horizontal). Up means more oxidizing; right means more alkaline. Each region shows the form of Cu that is most stable there.

The boundaries come in three kinds:

  • Horizontal: only electrons take part, so pH does not matter. Cu2+ + 2e− → Cu.
  • Vertical: only H+ takes part, so E does not matter. CuO + 2H+ → Cu2+ + H2O.
  • Sloped: both take part. When the numbers of H+ and e− are equal, E falls 0.0592 V per pH unit.

Warm-up with numbers you know 5 min

From Class 9: E(Cu2+/Cu) = 0.34 + 0.0296 log10[Cu2+]. The diagram is drawn for dissolved Cu at 10−6 M, a common choice for corrosion:

E = 0.34 + 0.0296 × (−6) = 0.162 V. This is the horizontal line between Cu and Cu2+.

From Class 5: pH = −log10[H+]; [H+] = 10−9 M is pH 9, alkaline, like a polishing slurry.

Check yourself 5 min

Write your answers first, then open each one.

1. Oxidation number of Cu in Cu2O and in CuO?

Answer+1 and +2.

2. [H+] = 10−9 M. What is the pH?

Answer9.

3. Find E(Cu2+/Cu) at 10−6 M.

Answer0.162 V.

4. Why is the Cu2+/Cu line horizontal?

AnswerOnly electrons take part, no H+, so pH does not change it.

5. On the diagram, is "up" more oxidizing or more reducing?

AnswerMore oxidizing (higher E).

6. Does a negative ΔG tell you how fast a reaction goes? (Classes 9 and 12)

AnswerNo: it says whether, not how fast. The same will hold for the diagram.
