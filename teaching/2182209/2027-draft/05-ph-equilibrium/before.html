The question for Class 5 3 min

A Cu plating bath is strongly acidic; the rinse water after it must be neutral; one wafer clean is basic. The concentration of H+ in these liquids ranges from about 1 mol/L to 10−12 mol/L. Class 5 introduces the pH scale for numbers this spread out, and the idea of equilibrium behind it.

Logarithms you need 6 min

log10 of a number is the power of ten that gives it:

The inverse is 10x: if log10 y = −4, then y = 10−4. Each step of 1 in the log is a factor of 10 in the number.

Acids and pH 7 min

An acid gives H+ ions in water; a base gives OH− ions (or takes up H+). A strong acid such as HCl gives up all its H+: HCl → H+ + Cl−, so 0.010 M HCl has [H+] = 0.010 mol/L. Square brackets mean concentration in mol/L.

pH = −log10[H+] and [H+] = 10−pH

[H+] = 10−2 gives pH 2; 10−4 gives pH 4. Note the direction: more acid means a lower pH. pH 2 has 100 times more H+ than pH 4, not twice as much.

Water has ions too 5 min

Even pure water splits slightly: H2O ⇌ H+ + OH−. At 25 °C the product of the two concentrations is fixed:

Kw = [H+][OH−] = 1.0 × 10−14

In pure water [H+] = [OH−] = 10−7 mol/L, so pH = 7: neutral. Below 7 is acidic, above 7 basic. If [H+] = 10−2, then [OH−] = 10−14 / 10−2 = 10−12 mol/L.

The double arrow ⇌ means the reaction runs both ways at once. That is the topic of Part 2 in class: equilibrium.
