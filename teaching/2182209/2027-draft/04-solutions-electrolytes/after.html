Two more worked examples

1. A smaller bath. Make 250.0 mL of 0.400 M CuSO4 from the blue crystals CuSO4·5H2O (249.7 g/mol).

n = 0.400 mol/L × 0.2500 L = 0.100 mol; m = 0.100 × 249.7 = 25.0 g

Dissolve 25.0 g in less than 250 mL of water, then fill to the 250.0 mL mark.

2. A bath recipe in g/L. A plating recipe asks for 40 g/L of Cu2+. In mol/L:

40 g/L ÷ 63.55 g/mol = 0.63 mol/L

That is inside the 0.3 to 0.8 mol/L range of acid copper baths for chip wiring. Plants often write g/L or mg/L; divide by M to get mol/L.

Three mistakes to avoid

  1. Adding V2 of water. V2 is the final volume. Add stock first, then water up to the mark.
  2. Weighing the hydrate as if it were CuSO4. The 5 H2O are part of each crystal unit; using 159.6 instead of 249.7 g/mol leaves the bath 36% too weak.
  3. "Deeper blue conducts better." The blue shows Cu2+ only. Conductivity depends on all the ions and must be measured.

Practice

Work on paper. The first four repeat the class; the rest go one step further. M in g/mol: Cu 63.55, Cl 35.45, CuSO4·5H2O 249.7.

1. What mass of CuSO4·5H2O makes 100.0 mL of 0.100 M?

Answer0.0100 mol × 249.7 = 2.50 g.

2. Give the ion concentrations in 0.0500 M Na2SO4.

Answer[Na+] = 0.100 M, [SO42−] = 0.0500 M. Charge: +0.100 − 2 × 0.0500 = 0.

3. 10.0 mL of 1.00 M CuSO4 is diluted to 250.0 mL. Find the new C.

Answer1.00 × 10.0 / 250.0 = 0.0400 M.

4. Convert 60 mg/L of Cl− to mol/L.

Answer0.060 g/L ÷ 35.45 = 1.69 × 10−3 mol/L.

5. What mass of the hydrate makes 500.0 mL of 0.150 M CuSO4?

Answern = 0.150 × 0.5000 = 0.0750 mol; 0.0750 × 249.7 = 18.7 g.

6. A recipe asks for 40 g/L of Cu2+. Convert to mol/L, and find the hydrate needed for 1.00 L.

Answer0.63 mol/L; 0.63 × 249.7 = 157 g.

7. A bath is 0.250 M CuSO4. How many g/L of Cu2+ is that?

Answer0.250 × 63.55 = 15.9 g/L.

8. A student forgets the 5 H2O and weighs 39.9 g of the hydrate for 1.00 L of 0.250 M. What concentration does the bath actually have?

Answer39.9 / 249.7 = 0.160 mol in 1.00 L: 0.160 M, 36% below 0.250 M.
