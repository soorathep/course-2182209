Two more worked examples

1. A Zn–Ag cell. Zn | Zn2+ || Ag+ | Ag. Zn is above Ag in the order of metals, so Zn is oxidized (anode) and Ag+ is reduced (cathode). Each Zn gives 2 electrons and each Ag+ takes 1, so the overall reaction is Zn + 2 Ag+ → Zn2+ + 2 Ag. Electrons flow from Zn to Ag in the wire; in the salt bridge NO3− moves toward the Zn side.

2. A LiFePO4 cell. Many electric cars use LiFePO4 instead of LiCoO2. With Li +1 and phosphate PO43−: in LiFePO4, (+1) + x + (−3) = 0, so Fe is +2. In FePO4, x + (−3) = 0, so Fe is +3. On discharge, Li+ and an electron enter FePO4, and Fe is reduced from +3 to +2; on charge, the reverse.

Three mistakes to avoid

  1. "The anode is always the negative electrode." The anode is where oxidation happens. During charging it is the positive electrode.
  2. "Electrons flow through the electrolyte." Electrons go through the wire; ions go through the electrolyte.
  3. "The salt bridge supplies the electrons." It supplies ions, to keep each side neutral.

Practice

Work on paper. The first four repeat the class; the rest go one step further. M in g/mol: Zn 65.38, O2 32.00, ZnO 81.38.

1. In a Li-ion cell in discharge, which way does Li+ move, and which electrode is the anode?

AnswerFrom graphite to LiCoO2; graphite is the anode.

2. Combine Zn + 2OH− → ZnO + H2O + 2e− with O2 + 2H2O + 4e− → 4OH−.

Answer2 Zn + O2 → 2 ZnO, 4 electrons; OH− and H2O cancel.

3. When a Li-ion cell charges, is its anode positive or negative?

AnswerPositive: oxidation happens at the LiCoO2 electrode.

4. In the Daniell cell, which way do NO3− ions move in the salt bridge, and why?

AnswerToward the Zn side, where Zn2+ builds up and positive charge must be balanced.

5. Write the cell notation and overall reaction for a Zn–Ag cell.

AnswerZn | Zn2+ || Ag+ | Ag; Zn + 2 Ag+ → Zn2+ + 2 Ag.

6. Find the oxidation number of Fe in LiFePO4 and FePO4.

Answer+2 and +3.

7. A zinc-air cell uses 2.00 g of Zn. Find the O2 taken from the air, the ZnO formed, and the moles of electrons.

Answern(Zn) = 0.0306 mol; O2 0.0153 mol = 0.489 g; ZnO 0.0306 mol = 2.49 g; electrons 0.0612 mol.

8. In Cu electroplating, which terminal of the power supply is the wafer joined to, and what happens at the Cu anode?

AnswerThe negative terminal: the wafer is the cathode, Cu2+ + 2e− → Cu. The Cu anode dissolves, Cu → Cu2+ + 2e−, and keeps the bath topped up.
