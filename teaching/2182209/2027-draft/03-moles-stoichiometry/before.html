Moles, units and chemical stoichiometry

About 30 minutes. Read it before Class 3, try the questions, and bring what is still unclear.
Download PDFSlidesLabAfter class

The question for Class 3 3 min

Classes 1 and 2 counted atoms one at a time. A real wafer holds about 1024 Si atoms, so nobody counts them; chemists weigh them. Class 3 links a mass you can put on a balance to a number of atoms, and then uses a reaction equation to find how much product forms. The example is the oxide grown on a Si wafer.

The mole is a count 6 min

A dozen means 12 things. A mole means NA = 6.022 × 1023 things, the Avogadro number. It is just a large counting unit.

The mole was chosen so that one mole of an element has a mass in grams equal to its atomic mass in u from Class 1. One Si atom is 28.09 u, so one mole of Si is 28.09 g. This mass per mole is the molar mass M, in g/mol.

n = m / M (moles from grams) and N = n × NA (atoms from moles)

Example. 1.00 g of Si: n = 1.00 g ÷ 28.09 g/mol = 0.0356 mol, and N = 0.0356 × 6.022 × 1023 = 2.14 × 1022 atoms.

Molar mass from a formula 5 min

Multiply each atomic mass by its subscript and add. Use M(Si) = 28.09, M(O) = 16.00, M(Cu) = 63.55, M(S) = 32.07 g/mol.

M(SiO2) = 28.09 + 2 × 16.00 = 60.09 g/mol

M(CuSO4) = 63.55 + 32.07 + 4 × 16.00 = 159.62 g/mol

Units first. M is in g/mol, so the mass must be in grams before you divide: 562 mg = 0.562 g.

A balanced equation counts particles 6 min

Si + O2 → SiO2 is balanced: 1 Si and 2 O on each side. The coefficients (here 1, 1, 1) say how many particles, or how many moles, react. They do not give masses: 1 mol of Si (28.1 g) reacts with 1 mol of O2 (32.0 g).

To balance an equation, change only the coefficients in front, never the subscripts in a formula. For example, 2 H2 + O2 → 2 H2O has 4 H and 2 O on each side.

The route for every problem: grams → moles → mole ratio from the equation → moles → grams

Density 3 min

Density ρ = mass / volume, in g/cm³. For Si it is 2.329 g/cm³. In class you will predict this number from the Si unit cell of Class 2, which joins the atom picture to the mole.

Check yourself 5 min

Write your answers first, then open each one.

1. How many atoms are in 2.00 mol of Cu?

Answer2.00 × 6.022 × 1023 = 1.20 × 1024.

2. How many moles are in 56.2 g of Si? Use 28.1 g/mol.

Answer56.2 / 28.1 = 2.00 mol.

3. Find M(SiO2) with Si 28.1 and O 16.0 g/mol.

Answer28.1 + 2 × 16.0 = 60.1 g/mol.

4. A classmate gets 2.00 mol from 56.2 mg of Si. Find the error.

Answer56.2 mg is 0.0562 g, so n = 0.00200 mol. Convert mg to g first.

5. Balance: H2 + O2 → H2O.

Answer2 H2 + O2 → 2 H2O.

6. In Si + O2 → SiO2, does 1 g of Si react with 1 g of O2?

AnswerNo. 1 mol reacts with 1 mol; 28.1 g of Si with 32.0 g of O2.

If you got 5 or 6 right, you are ready. If not, reread the section on the mole.
