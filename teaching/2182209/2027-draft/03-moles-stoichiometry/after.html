Two more worked examples

1. The density of Cu from its unit cell. Cu is face-centered cubic: 4 atoms per cell (Class 2), edge a = 0.3615 nm = 3.615 × 10−8 cm.

Mass of one cell = 4 × 63.55 g/mol ÷ 6.022 × 1023 mol−1 = 4.221 × 10−22 g

Volume of one cell = (3.615 × 10−8 cm)3 = 4.724 × 10−23 cm³

ρ = 4.221 × 10−22 / 4.724 × 10−23 = 8.93 g/cm³, close to the measured 8.96 g/cm³. Class 10 uses this density.

2. Silicon nitride from silane. Chips use Si3N4 films made from SiH4 and NH3: 3 SiH4 + 4 NH3 → Si3N4 + 12 H2. What mass of Si3N4 can 1.00 g of SiH4 give?

M(SiH4) = 28.09 + 4 × 1.008 = 32.12 g/mol, so n = 1.00 / 32.12 = 0.0311 mol

Mole ratio: 3 SiH4 give 1 Si3N4, so n(Si3N4) = 0.0311 / 3 = 0.0104 mol

M(Si3N4) = 3 × 28.09 + 4 × 14.01 = 140.3 g/mol, so m = 0.0104 × 140.3 = 1.46 g

Three mistakes to avoid

  1. Dividing milligrams by g/mol. Convert to grams first; otherwise the answer is 1000 times too large.
  2. Reading coefficients as masses. 1 : 1 in Si + O2 → SiO2 is a ratio of moles, not of grams.
  3. "The product is heavier, so mass was created." Count everything that enters, including the O2 from the gas.

Practice

Work on paper. The first four repeat the class; the rest go one step further. M in g/mol: H 1.008, N 14.01, O 16.00, Si 28.09, Cu 63.55. NA = 6.022 × 1023 mol−1.

1. How many Cu atoms are in 1.00 µg of Cu?

Answer1.00 × 10−6 g / 63.55 = 1.57 × 10−8 mol, times NA = 9.48 × 1015 atoms.

2. 1.00 g of Si is oxidized completely. Find the masses of SiO2 formed and O2 used.

Answern = 1.00/28.09 = 0.0356 mol. SiO2: 0.0356 × 60.09 = 2.14 g. O2: 0.0356 × 32.00 = 1.14 g. Check: 1.00 + 1.14 = 2.14 g.

3. About how many nm of Si are used up when 200 nm of SiO2 grows?

Answer0.44 × 200 = 88 nm.

4. What is the mass gain when 200 nm of SiO2 grows on 1 cm²?

AnswerTwice the 100 nm value: about 23.4 µg.

5. Find M(Si3N4).

Answer3 × 28.09 + 4 × 14.01 = 140.3 g/mol.

6. A Cu film is 1.0 µm thick on 1.0 cm² (ρ = 8.96 g/cm³). How many Cu atoms does it hold?

AnswerVolume 1.0 × 10−4 cm³; mass 8.96 × 10−4 g; 1.41 × 10−5 mol; 8.5 × 1018 atoms.

7. Balance: Si + Cl2 → SiCl4. What mass of Cl2 (70.90 g/mol) reacts with 2.81 g of Si?

AnswerSi + 2 Cl2 → SiCl4. n(Si) = 0.100 mol, so n(Cl2) = 0.200 mol = 14.2 g.

8. Can the numbers above tell you how long a 100 nm oxide takes to grow? Why?

AnswerNo. Stoichiometry gives amounts, not rates. The time depends on temperature, on O2 or H2O, and on diffusion through the oxide already grown.
