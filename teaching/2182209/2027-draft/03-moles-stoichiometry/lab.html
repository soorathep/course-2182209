The oxide on a silicon wafer

About 30 minutes. Works in class or on your own, on a laptop or a phone. Nothing to install; a calculator helps.

How this lab works

A Si wafer heated in dry O2 grows a layer of insulating SiO2: Si + O2 → SiO2. You follow the masses and moles of this reaction, count atoms, calculate the density of Si from its unit cell, and find how much Si an oxide layer uses up. Write each prediction before you look; the next step stays locked until you do. After two tries the expected value is shown.

What runs where. The expected values come from stoich_lab.py, computed in advance. The slider in Task 4 does one small calculation in JavaScript, the same formula as the Python. No Python is installed or run on your device. To run the Python yourself, open the slides and press D on a slide marked “D · Python”, or run stoich_lab.py in desktop Python.

Write very large or small numbers as 1.23e22 (meaning 1.23 × 1022). Studying on your own? Watch the narrated slides for Class 3 first.
